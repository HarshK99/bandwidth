# Founder operating system

## 1. North star

### Wave Link
- Primary strategic objective: grow by **5% every week**.
- Purpose: build the ability to grow a business from zero.
- Scoreboard: meaningful/active users, not vanity registrations.

### Income
- Hard financial constraint: **≥₹60k/month**.
- Requirement: reliably generate ₹60k/month starting next month.
- Current bridge: existing website receivables expected this month.

### Hierarchy
1. Wave Link gets to win strategically.
2. ₹60k/month income must not fail.
3. Content amplifies both.
4. Side projects remain optional.
5. Jobs remain low-effort optionality.
6. Sleep, exercise and recovery remain protected.

### Core operating loop
**Outcome → current bottleneck → highest-leverage bets → allocate time → execute → measure → learn → next bottleneck**

The calendar serves the strategy. The strategy does not serve the calendar.

### One-bottleneck principle
There may be many possible bottlenecks, but only the **current bottleneck** gets focused attention at a time.

When the bottleneck changes, the work and calendar change.

## 2. Operating principles

### Focus
- One thing gets to win; this does not mean only one activity is allowed.
- Wave Link is the strategic winner.
- ₹60k/month is the financial constraint.
- Everything else must justify its use of capacity.
- New ideas do not automatically become new goals.

### Metrics dictate work
For Wave Link:

- Work on that constraint first.

For income:
- Target: ≥₹60k/month.
- Ask: **What is preventing reliable ₹60k cash generation?**
- Work on that constraint first.

For content:

- Positioning

## 3. Freelance niche strategy

### Niche is an experiment, not an identity
The first niche does not need to be permanent. The goal is to find a market where the probability of acquiring ₹25k+ clients is high enough to justify focused effort.

Evaluate:
- Customer value
- Website economic impact
- Ability to pay ₹25k+
- Visible website problems
- Prospect volume
- Reachable decision-makers
- Urgency/reason to change
- Visual proof potential
- Referral potential
- Content potential
- Adjacent/repeat work
- Ease of acquisition

Economic value matters more than geography.

### Niche validation sequence
**Niche hypothesis → market investigation → evidence → choose niche → define offer → acquire first clients → measure → next bottleneck**

Do not research a niche indefinitely. Move on when enough evidence exists to make a good-enough decision.

A strong niche signal is the overlap of:
**website matters + current presence is inadequate + owner recognizes the problem + willingness to pay**.

A bad website alone is not a business problem.

### Current niche: dental clinics
**Status: current niche hypothesis; move forward aggressively enough to test, but do not treat it as permanent.**

Evidence so far:
- A dentist friend confirmed that dental clinics want websites.
- A warm referral path exists through dentist relationships.
- Some dental clinic websites observed are outdated or absent.
- User can build the websites and can target ₹25k+.
- A referral arrangement has been discussed: 20% of the fee for clients directly brought by the dentist friend.

What remains unproven:
- How broadly the need exists across dental clinics.
- What specific business job the website performs.
- Whether clinic owners recognize the problem strongly enough to pay ₹25k+.
- How many prospects can be acquired independently.
- Whether the niche can support roughly 3 projects/month.

### Dental discovery questions
Talk to dentists/clinic owners before optimizing the sales pitch. Learn:
- How do new patients find the clinic?
- Do patients visit the website before contacting/booking?
- What do patients look for online?
- What is the current website used for?
- What is wrong or outdated about it?
- Have they considered rebuilding it? Why or why not?
- What would make a new website worth paying ₹25k–₹40k?
- Who decides and pays for the website?
- If the website disappeared tomorrow, would the business materially suffer?

The objective of these conversations is **market understanding first, selling second**.

### Dental acquisition hypothesis
Potential flywheel:
**dentist referral → first clinic → strong work/proof → clinic-owner referrals → niche credibility → direct outreach/content → more clinics**

Referral partners are an acquisition channel, not the entire business. The long-term goal is repeatable acquisition that does not depend on one friend.

### Current freelance offer hypothesis
**“I build modern, premium websites for businesses that make them look more credible and make it easier for visitors to take the next step.”**

For dental clinics, sharpen this only after discovery.

Current capability supports:
- Modern/premium design.
- Credibility/trust presentation.
- Clear information architecture.
- Easier patient next steps.

Do not yet promise:
- SEO rankings.
- Guaranteed leads.
- Guaranteed revenue.
- Conversion improvements without evidence.

Lead-generation/conversion expertise is a direction to learn into through real projects, not a claim to sell before it is proven.

## 7. Exploration and side projects

**Idea → small experiment → evidence → stronger opportunity → earn more time**

No evidence → park it.
Evidence → consider activating it.

Three states:
- **Idea:** interesting, no commitment.
- **Experiment:** small, time-boxed, tests demand/economics.
- **Active business:** evidence exists and ongoing resources are justified.

Explore everything mentally. Execute very few things.

## 8. AI workflow

Use AI for leverage.

Improve tooling when it removes a real bottleneck.

Do not:
- Spend weeks perfecting tooling before getting clients.
- Build infrastructure because it feels productive.
- Learn technology without a demonstrated need.

**Bottleneck first. Infrastructure second.**

## 9. Time, energy and recovery

### Capacity
- Up to 10 hours/day.
- Up to 6 working days/week.
- Up to 60 hours/week.
- 60 hours is a ceiling, not a quota.

Do not fill unused capacity with low-value work.

### Energy philosophy
Use the founder's observed energy pattern as a starting hypothesis, not a permanent schedule.

**High-energy periods**
- Reserve for work where **thinking quality materially matters**:
  - Customer conversations
  - ICP / customer discovery
  - Positioning
  - Sales
  - Strategy
  - Important decisions
  - High-value writing
  - Work where better reasoning can materially change the outcome

**Lower-energy periods**
- Prefer work where peak cognitive sharpness matters less:
  - Lead research
  - Website production
  - Editing
  - Posting
  - Admin
  - Routine analytics
  - Revisions

The purpose is not to create a rigid timetable. Match the quality of thinking required by the work to the energy available.

Current observed pattern:
- 8:00–11:00: peak
- 11:00–13:00: strong
- 14:00–15:30: sleepier
- 15:30–17:00: improving
- 17:00–18:00: break
- 18:00–19:30: good
- 21:00–22:00: gym
- After gym: recovery, hobbies and light planning

If evidence shows the pattern or task fit is wrong, change it.

### Recovery
Non-negotiables:
- At least 7 hours sleep.
- 1 hour exercise.
- Proper meals.
- Downtime.
- Hobbies.
- Evening wind-down.

Preferred boundary: no serious work after the gym.
Desired bedtime: move toward midnight.

### Context switching
Prefer larger blocks, grouped freelance work, batched content and protected Wave Link blocks. But themes remain subordinate to outcomes.

## 11. Weekly operating cadence

### Start of week
1. Check Wave Link growth.
2. Identify the current Wave Link bottleneck.
3. Choose the highest-leverage Wave Link bets.
4. Check freelance cash and pipeline.
5. Identify what is required to reach ≥₹60k/month.
6. Identify the current freelance bottleneck.
7. Choose the highest-leverage freelance bets.
8. Decide which content supports those bets.
9. Park nonessential opportunities.
10. Allocate work into low-context-switching blocks.
11. Protect recovery.

### End of week
1. Measure Wave Link growth.
2. Review what was learned.
3. Measure freelance pipeline and cash.
4. Review content results.
5. Identify the next bottleneck.
6. Adjust the following week's allocation.

### Planning hierarchy
**Quarter → direction**
- Major outcomes and strategic direction.

**Month → strategy**
- What matters most given current evidence and constraints.

**Week → execution**
- Current bottlenecks and highest-leverage bets.

**Day → energy + execution**
- Put the week's work into the day's energy windows.

The schedule is dynamic. Do not create one fixed weekly schedule and follow it indefinitely.

### The real-life operating model

School often gives a known sequence:
**Understand → learn the method → follow steps → answer**

Business is different:
**Hypothesis → action → evidence → update → next action**

When there is no obvious next step:
1. What am I trying to learn or achieve?
2. What is the smallest action that gives useful evidence?
3. What will I decide based on that evidence?

**Uncertainty → small experiment → evidence → clarity → action**

Research is useful when it reduces uncertainty. Research that replaces action is usually avoidance.
